# Running a Genesis Session

A Genesis Session is the first cycle of [the Gameplay Loop](../concepts/The%20Gameplay%20Loop.md): a four-hour session that builds the universe the campaign will be played in. The players are gods. They make themselves, make the world together, and then create it one piece at a time, speaking each creation aloud in character and writing it down out of character. Every creation adds Entropy to a debt, and before the night ends the gods spend that debt on the Doom the world will face. Each god hides a relic for their future mortal self. Then everyone becomes a mortal who knows none of it, and the campaign begins: recover the relics, and use them together to soften the Doom.

It runs for any number of players from two to eight. The default below is four. The result is the founding canon of a new universe (see [Adding a Universe](Adding%20a%20Universe.md)); the printable sheets are in [Genesis Session Handouts](Genesis%20Session%20Handouts.md). Every later cycle is a shorter [Convocation](Running%20a%20Convocation.md).

Everything here is a base way, not the right way. Options are marked. Change what you like; only the Omniverse's note shapes are fixed, and only when you merge up.

## What you need
- A **Scribe**: the facilitator, usually the DM. Keeps the Ledger and the Entropy tally, runs the clock, holds the secrets, rules on anchors. See *The Scribe* below for the three ways to play it.
- 2 to 8 **gods**: the players.
- The handouts, printed or on screens: the Rules card, the Loop card, a God Sheet each, Act Cards, Pact Slips, Relic Slips, the Reckoning Sheet, an Incarnation Sheet each, and the Ledger.
- A timer, and something to write on.

## The big ideas
1. **Yes, and.** Nothing a god has made can be deleted or contradicted. If two creations clash, both are true somewhere, and the clash becomes a feature.
2. **Every creation is anchored.** It connects to something that already exists, so the world is one web rather than a pile.
3. **Every creation is marked.** It shows a fingerprint of its maker's Domain, so the world reads as a pantheon's work.
4. **Deals, not force.** Nobody can stop another god from creating. They can only make an offer, and the other god may say no.
5. **Creation costs.** Every creation adds Entropy: the World 5, Grand 3, Middling 2, Small 1. The debt is public, and the gods must spend it on the Doom. Entropy is necessary and regrettable, nobody's mistake; the gods go down as mortals to soften it, never to undo it.
6. **The Veil.** What the gods know stays in the Ledger. Avatars know only what mortals in that world would know. And gods cannot touch the world between Convocations except through their avatars.

## The Scribe
Three ways to play it; pick one before the session.
- **Scribe only** (base): runs the table and creates nothing.
- **God only**: makes a god and creates at Genesis and every Convocation, but never incarnates; DMs the mortal phases. Takes the Doom-Keeper duty by default (see *The Reckoning*). The natural choice for a DM at a small table.
- **God and avatar**: plays both, for tables of three or fewer where the DM would otherwise sit out the fun. Their relic goes on the honor system.

## Shape of the session

| Phase | What happens | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Apotheosis | Each god claims a Domain and a Sign; the First Word is spoken; the table makes the World together | 35 min |
| Round 1: Foundation | Each god makes one Grand creation | scaled |
| Rounds 2 to R−1: Weaving | Each god makes one Middling or Small creation per round | scaled |
| Break | Ten minutes, somewhere in the Weaving | 10 min |
| Final round: Relics | Each god hides a relic and says a public Proverb | scaled |
| Reckoning | The gods spend the Entropy debt on the Doom | 20 min |
| Incarnation | Each god builds their avatar; the Veil comes down | 25 min + 4 per god |
| Close | The Proverbs and the Doom's public face are read aloud; the Ledger is sealed | 5 min |

### Scaling to the number of gods
Rounds scale so the table makes about 20 creations whatever its size, and so owes about 35 Entropy. An Act (one god's turn) takes about six minutes, five with seven or eight gods. The spare time is for deals.

| Gods | Rounds (R) | Weaving rounds | Total Acts | Minutes per Act | Spare minutes for deals | Expected debt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 10 | 8 | 20 | 6 | 17 | about 35 |
| 3 | 7 | 5 | 21 | 6 | 7 | about 36 |
| **4** | **5** | **3** | **20** | **6** | **9** | **about 35** |
| 5 | 4 | 2 | 20 | 6 | 5 | about 35 |
| 6 | 3 | 1 | 18 | 6 | 13 | about 32 |
| 7 | 3 | 1 | 21 | 5 | 12 | about 36 |
| 8 | 3 | 1 | 24 | 5 | −7 | about 41 |

The spare column assumes the fixed parts above (Apotheosis 35, break 10, Reckoning 20, Incarnation 25 plus 4 per god, close 5). The spare is thin: keep Acts to time, and if deals run long, push the Reckoning to the start of the first campaign session. With eight gods plan four and a half hours. The expected debt assumes Weaving Acts split evenly between Middling and Small; a greedy table owes more.

**Turn order.** The first round runs in the order the Domains were claimed. Each round reverses the order, so nobody always goes last.

## Apotheosis (35 minutes)
Making a god costs nothing.
1. The Scribe reads the Opening (in the handouts). The table is empty: no light, no ground, no names.
2. Each god fills in a God Sheet: a **Name**, a **Domain** (a noun or idea, two words at most: *Tides*, *Bargains*, *Forgotten Things*), a **Sign** (an image or symbol), and a **Voice** (how the god speaks: grand, petty, sleepy, exact). Domains must be different; near-duplicates are fine if the gods can explain the difference in character.
3. The **First Word**: going around once, each god says one sentence that is true before anything exists, in character ("There was an owing before there was anything to owe"). The Scribe writes them as the first lines of the Ledger. They are canon, and they set the tone.
4. **The World.** The whole table makes the world as one Joint Act: going around once more, each god adds one line (what the sky is, what the ground is, what the sea is, what is strange about it). It costs 5 Entropy, the first entry on the tally, and the Scribe writes the planet note. From here on, anyone can populate it alone.
   - *Option:* a second world or a plane beyond the sky, another Joint Act at 5, for tables that want to travel between them.
   - *Option (ascended gods):* at a later cycle a player may take an ascended avatar as their god instead of making a new one; see *Retirement* in the loop.

## An Act (the unit of play)
On your turn you take one Act. Follow the steps in order; each takes seconds.

1. **Declare the size and the thing.** Grand, Middling or Small (see below), and what it is. The size is the cost.
2. **Speak the Word.** One or two sentences, in character, as the god saying it into being. This is the in-world voice; the table may react in character.
3. **Anchor and Mark.** Say what existing thing it connects to, and how your Domain touches it. Both are one line.
4. **Yes-and.** Each other god may add one detail in one sentence. Two adds at most.
5. **The Note.** Out of character, the Scribe writes it in the Ledger: a name, one line of description, one detail useful at the table. The god confirms.
6. **The Tally.** The Scribe adds the weight to the Entropy debt, out loud.

### Sizes and weights
- **Grand (3)**: a plane, a continent, a new people or race, a law of reality (magic works like this, death works like this), a god-scale force.
- **Middling (2)**: a region, a country, a city, a faction or religion, a monster species, an era or historical event, a language, a holiday.
- **Small (1)**: a person, a relic or item, a place inside a place (a tavern, a ruin, a tree), a rumour, a song, a recipe, a grudge.

Round 1 is all Grand. Weaving rounds are Middling or Small, whichever you like, and across the session each god must make at least one Small thing. (The Act Cards in the handouts have prompts for each size.)

### Joint Acts
Two gods can each spend one Weaving Act to make one Grand thing together. It costs 3, both speak a Word, and both Marks appear.

### When creations clash
Do not delete. The Scribe asks the two gods to explain, in character, how both are true. The usual answers: different eras, different peoples' beliefs, or a place where the contradiction itself is the point. Write the answer in the Ledger as lore. Clashes are good raw material for the Doom.

## Deals
A god can never stop another god from creating. A god can **Petition**: ask another god, before that god declares their Act, not to make a particular thing.

- **The standard price is two for one.** The petitioner **gifts two** of their own unspent Weaving Acts to the other god, who forgoes the one Act or creation they were going to make. The target then has extra Acts to spend, and every gifted Act carries the donor's Mark, so the donor still leaves a fingerprint on the world. Weights do not change hands: an Act costs what its size costs, whoever spends it.
- **Other prices are fine.** Any offer both gods accept works: a promise, a boon in the world, a swapped Domain privilege, a relic shared. The two-for-one is only the default when the gods cannot think of anything better.
- **The target may refuse.** Then the Act goes ahead, and the petitioner gets one **Caveat**: a single cost or limit the creation must carry. The creator may reword the Caveat in their own voice but must keep its teeth. The creation is never cancelled.
  - *Option (Caveat spends 1):* a Caveat spends 1 Entropy from the debt, since its cost was paid up front.
- **Every deal is a Pact.** Write it on a Pact Slip, read it in character and give it to the Scribe. Pacts are public. They become Fates later, and they come due at the next Convocation.
- **Limits.** Only Weaving Acts can be traded. A god's Foundation Act and their relic are theirs alone, so everyone has a voice at the start and a secret at the end. With six or more gods there is only one Weaving round, so the standard price is one Act plus a Pact.

## Relics and Proverbs (final round)
Each god's final Act has two quick parts, both done in the same turn. Neither costs Entropy.
- A **Relic**: one thing hidden in the world for your future avatar. Fill in a Relic Slip, privately, and give it to the Scribe: what it is; what it does alone, in its bearer's hands (a minor power, in the campaign's ruleset); what it adds when joined with the others; where it is hidden, anchored to something already made; and how an avatar might come to it. Nobody else at the table learns what it is, only that it was hidden. A relic wakes only for its own god's avatar; anyone can carry it.
  - *Option:* a relic may be a person, a place or a truth instead of an object. It still wakes, and it still joins.
- A **Proverb**: one sentence mortals in this world say about it, said aloud. Proverbs are the public record. They, and the Doom's public face, are the only lore the avatars start with.

## The Reckoning (20 minutes)
The Scribe reads the tally. That is the debt, and the gods must spend it now on the Doom. Creation leaked; this is what it leaked. No one is at fault, and no one may refuse. The full menu and the Convergence terms are in [the Gameplay Loop](../concepts/The%20Gameplay%20Loop.md#the-doom) and on the Reckoning Sheet.

**Who shapes it.** Decide at the start of the session.
- **Council** (base): the whole table shapes it now. The Scribe reads out the Ledger's sore spots (the Caveats, the broken Pacts, the clashes); then the gods pick one Scale, one Form, and Features until the debt is spent, each pick proposed in character by whoever wants it and agreed by the table. The Scribe writes the true Doom. Gods know all of it; avatars know none.
- **Doom-Keeper**: one god takes the duty, by default the Scribe's own god. The Keeper takes the Ledger home and shapes the Doom privately, spending the same debt on the same menu, and decides how the relics join. At the start of the first campaign session they reveal only the public face. The other gods know only their own relic.
- **Mixed**: Council shapes the Doom; the Keeper decides the Convergence.

**Then, whoever shaped it:**
1. **The public face.** Write what mortals believe about what is coming, in one paragraph: a prophecy, a rumour, a sermon. It may be wrong (and must be, if the Doom has a Mask). This is read aloud at the Close.
2. **The Convergence terms** come from the Scale: anywhere; at one place; at one place at one moment; or that plus a price one bearer pays. The Keeper or the Council chooses the place and the moment; the avatars discover them.
3. **Remainder.** Entropy too small to spend carries forward to the next Reckoning.

- *Option (a Domain turned):* the Doom wears the Domain of the god who added the most Entropy, gone wrong.
- *Option (no economy):* skip the tally and pick the Doom freely.

## Incarnation (25 minutes plus 4 per god)
Seal the Ledger. From here the gods are gone; the Scribe holds the book.

1. **Concept.** Each player makes an **avatar** of their own god: the god incarnate, who remembers nothing. The concept can be anything that fits the world. (*Option:* a chosen one instead, a mortal the god picked; the rules are the same.)
2. **Stats.** Build them in the universe's chosen ruleset (Tropelandia uses [D&D 5e](../../Rulesets)). Genesis does not change the system.
3. **Echo.** Write one **Echo**: a pull or instinct from the god's Domain that the avatar cannot explain (the sea-goddess who never feels cold; the god of bargains who always knows a price). Once per session, when acting on the Echo, you take a mild edge. In 5e that is advantage on one roll; in other systems a bonus die or similar. When your relic wakes, the Echo becomes a real power, as written on the Relic Slip.
4. **Fate.** The Scribe reads out the Pacts and gives each player a **Fate**: a consequence of a Pact their god made, felt by the avatar as luck, a debt, an enemy, a dream. The Scribe decides the shape; the player decides how the avatar copes with it.
5. **The Lead.** The Scribe gives each avatar one Lead to their relic, drawn from the Relic Slip: a dream, an heirloom, a rumour, a scar. The Lead is the first stage of the Relic Quest.
6. **The Veil comes down.** Players now know only their avatar's life, the Proverbs, the public face of the Doom, and anything the avatar would plausibly know. If unsure, ask the Scribe: "does my avatar know this?" Players who remember their own god's creations leave them unspoken in character. Clues to relics and Marks come from play.

### Resonance
When an avatar meets something marked by their own god (a relic, a Mark, one of their creations), the Scribe calls a **Resonance**: a dream, a chill, a coincidence. It is a story beat, not a mechanic, but it is also when the Echo is easiest to use, and it is how the Relic Quest gives its clues.

### Death and rebirth in play
When an avatar dies mid-campaign, the player's god is reborn at once as a new avatar who joins the party. The player keeps a **Remnant**: one fact the old life learned, witnessed by the Scribe, that the new life dimly knows. Names and faces are gone; lessons stay. Echo and Fate carry over, and the relic wakes again for the new avatar, since it is the same god. The ends available when a campaign closes are in [the Gameplay Loop](../concepts/The%20Gameplay%20Loop.md#retirement).

## After the session: into the Omniverse
The sealed Ledger is the universe's founding canon.
1. Scaffold the universe: `python3 _rules/tools/new_universe.py "<Name>" --tone "<tone>" --ruleset <ruleset id> --maintainer <handle>`.
2. Each god becomes a `deity` note in `Pantheon/` (name, domains, symbol, tenets drawn from the First Word and Marks, `allies`/`rivals` from the Pacts).
3. The World is the planet note; Grand and Middling creations become the place tree, factions, eras, languages and holidays; Small ones become people, items, lore and events dated `primordial`.
4. The Doom's public face is a `lore` note (`category: prophecy`). The true Doom and the relics live in the campaign's instance, kept `visibility: private` until the avatars find them.
5. Create the instance for the campaign and fork it at the frontier. Pacts become Fates in the characters' overlay notes; the Entropy tally and the Doom's outcome go in the campaign's `arc`.
6. Validate and build as usual (`validate.py`, `build_all.py`). An LLM can do steps 2 to 5 from the Ledger; see [Contributing with an LLM](Contributing%20with%20an%20LLM.md).

Prefer to do it yourselves? The [Note Wizard](../getting-started/The%20Note%20Wizard.md) turns each Ledger entry into a valid note: pick the kind, fill in the blanks, and save the whole basket into the new universe's folder. The where-everything-lands table is in [the Gameplay Loop](../concepts/The%20Gameplay%20Loop.md#where-each-phase-lands-in-the-repository).

## Scribe's checklist
- Before: print the handouts; pick a ruleset; decide the tone (a parody, grim, mythic); decide how you will play the Scribe and who shapes the Doom; read the Opening aloud once to yourself.
- During: keep the Ledger and the tally current, call time at six minutes, keep the Veil, make sure every Act has an Anchor and a Mark, offer the clash rule instead of vetoing.
- Midway: say the debt out loud now and then; remind quiet gods that Small Acts count and cost less, and that deals are available.
- Last round: collect the Relic Slips without reading them aloud.
- Reckoning: read the sore spots first; make the table spend it all.
- After: seal the Ledger; photograph it; start the universe.

## Variations
- **Short form (two hours):** two Acts per god (one Grand, one relic), the World, a Keeper's Doom shaped between sessions, no deals, a 20-minute Incarnation.
- **Mortal-first:** run the gods' rounds, then skip the Veil and let the gods play their avatars with full knowledge (a mythic, gods-among-us game).
- **Keeper between sessions:** end the Genesis Session at the Relics round and let the Keeper do the Reckoning at home. Buys back 20 minutes for deals.
- **Rival houses:** with seven or eight gods, split into two houses of four that make separate halves of the world, then run a Joint Act to join them.
- **No economy:** no weights, no tally; the Doom is picked freely. The loop still works; it just stops pushing back.
