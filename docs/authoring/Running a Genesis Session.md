# Running a Genesis Session

A Genesis Session is a first session that builds the universe the campaign will be played in. The players are gods. They take turns creating the world one piece at a time, speaking each creation aloud in character and then writing it down out of character. At the end each god becomes a mortal incarnation who knows none of it, and the campaign begins inside the world they made. When an incarnation dies, the god can be reborn into the next session.

It runs in four hours for any number of players from two to eight. The default below is four. The result is the founding canon of a new universe (see [Adding a Universe](Adding%20a%20Universe.md)); players get a printable set of cards from [Genesis Session Handouts](Genesis%20Session%20Handouts.md).

## What you need
- A **Scribe**: the facilitator (usually the DM). Keeps the Ledger, runs the clock, holds the secrets, rules on anchors. The Scribe does not create; the gods do.
- 2 to 8 **gods**: the players.
- The handouts, printed or on screens: the Rules card, a God Sheet each, Act Cards, Pact Slips, Seed Slips, an Incarnation Sheet each, and the Ledger.
- A timer, and something to write on.

## The big ideas
1. **Yes, and.** Nothing a god has made can be deleted or contradicted. If two creations clash, both are true somewhere, and the clash becomes a feature.
2. **Every creation is anchored.** It connects to something that already exists, so the world is one web rather than a pile.
3. **Every creation is marked.** It shows a fingerprint of its maker's domain, so the world reads as a pantheon's work.
4. **Deals, not force.** Nobody can stop another god from creating. They can only make an offer, and the other god may say no.
5. **The Veil.** What the gods know stays in the Ledger. Incarnations know only what mortals in that world would know.

## Shape of the session

| Phase | What happens | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Prologue | Each god claims a Domain and a Sign; the First Word is spoken together | 30 min |
| Round 1: Foundation | Each god makes one Grand creation | scaled |
| Rounds 2 to R-1: Weaving | Each god makes one Middling or Small creation per round | scaled |
| Final round: Seeds | Each god plants one secret Seed and writes one public Proverb | scaled |
| Break | Ten minutes, somewhere in the Weaving | 10 min |
| Incarnation | Each god builds their mortal; the Veil comes down | 30 min + 4 per god |
| Close | Read the Proverbs aloud; seal the Ledger | 10 min |

### Scaling to the number of gods
Rounds scale so the table makes about 20 creations whatever its size. An Act (one god's turn) takes about six minutes, five with seven or eight gods. The spare time is for deals.

| Gods | Rounds (R) | Weaving rounds | Total Acts | Minutes per Act | Spare minutes for deals |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 10 | 8 | 20 | 6 | 32 |
| 3 | 7 | 5 | 21 | 6 | 22 |
| **4** | **5** | **3** | **20** | **6** | **24** |
| 5 | 4 | 2 | 20 | 6 | 20 |
| 6 | 3 | 1 | 18 | 6 | 28 |
| 7 | 3 | 1 | 21 | 5 | 27 |
| 8 | 3 | 1 | 24 | 5 | 8 |

The spare column assumes the fixed parts above (prologue 30, incarnation 30 plus 4 per god, break 10, close 10). With eight gods the clock is tight; add 30 minutes or let the incarnation phase spill into the next sitting.

**Turn order.** The first round runs in the order the Domains were claimed. Each round reverses the order, so nobody always goes last.

## Prologue (30 minutes)
1. The Scribe reads the Opening (in the handouts). The table is empty: no light, no ground, no names.
2. Each god fills in a God Sheet: a **Name**, a **Domain** (a noun or idea, two words at most: *Tides*, *Bargains*, *Forgotten Things*), a **Sign** (an image or symbol), and a **Voice** (how the god speaks: grand, petty, sleepy, exact). Domains must be different; near-duplicates are fine if the gods can explain the difference in character.
3. The **First Word**: going around once, each god says one sentence that is true before anything exists, in character ("There was an owing before there was anything to owe"). The Scribe writes them as the first lines of the Ledger. They are canon, and they set the tone.

## An Act (the unit of play)
On your turn you take one Act. Follow the steps in order; each takes seconds.

1. **Declare the size and the thing.** Grand, Middling or Small (see below), and what it is.
2. **Speak the Word.** One or two sentences, in character, as the god saying it into being. This is the in-world voice; the table may react in character.
3. **Anchor and Mark.** Say what existing thing it connects to, and how your Domain touches it. Both are one line.
4. **Yes-and.** Each other god may add one detail in one sentence. Two adds at most.
5. **The Note.** Out of character, the Scribe writes it in the Ledger: a name, one line of description, one detail useful at the table. The god confirms.

### Sizes
- **Grand**: a plane, a world, a continent, a new people or race, a law of reality (magic works like this, death works like this), a god-scale force.
- **Middling**: a region, a country, a city, a faction or religion, a monster species, an era or historical event, a language, a holiday.
- **Small**: a person, a relic, a place inside a place (a tavern, a ruin, a tree), a rumour, a song, a recipe, a grudge.

Round 1 is all Grand. Weaving rounds are Middling or Small, whichever you like, and across the session each god must make at least one Small thing. Seeds are always Small. (The Act Cards in the handouts have prompts for each size.)

### Joint Acts
Two gods can each spend one Weaving Act to make one Grand thing together. They both speak a Word and both Marks appear.

### When creations clash
Do not delete. The Scribe asks the two gods to explain, in character, how both are true. The usual answers: different eras, different peoples' beliefs, or a place where the contradiction itself is the point. Write the answer in the Ledger as lore.

## Deals
A god can never stop another god from creating. A god can **Petition**: ask another god, before that god declares their Act, not to make a particular thing.

- **The standard price is two for one.** The petitioner **gifts two** of their own unspent Weaving Acts to the other god, who forgoes the one Act or creation they were going to make. The target then has extra Acts to spend, and every gifted Act carries the donor's Mark, so the donor still leaves a fingerprint on the world.
- **Other prices are fine.** Any offer both gods accept works: a promise, a boon in the world, a swapped Domain privilege, a Seed shared. The two-for-one is only the default when the gods cannot think of anything better.
- **The target may refuse.** Then the Act goes ahead, and the petitioner gets one **Caveat**: a single cost or limit the creation must carry. The creator may reword the Caveat in their own voice but must keep its teeth. The creation is never cancelled.
- **Every deal is a Pact.** Write it on a Pact Slip, read it in character and give it to the Scribe. Pacts are public. They become fates later.
- **Limits.** Only Weaving Acts can be traded. A god's Foundation Act and Seed Act are theirs alone, so everyone has a voice at the start and a secret at the end. With six or more gods there is only one Weaving round, so the standard price is one Act plus a Pact.

## Seeds and Proverbs (final round)
Each god's final Act has two quick parts, both done in the same turn:
- A **Seed**: a secret creation (a relic, a person, a truth, a place) anchored to something already made. It goes on a Seed Slip, privately, to the Scribe. Nobody else at the table learns what it is, only that it was planted. The Scribe places it in the campaign.
- A **Proverb**: one sentence mortals in this world say about it, said aloud. Proverbs are the public record. They are the only lore the incarnations start with.

## Incarnation (30 minutes plus 4 per god)
Seal the Ledger. From here the gods are gone; the Scribe holds the book.

1. **Concept.** Each player makes a mortal who is an incarnation of their own god. The concept can be anything that fits the world.
2. **Stats.** Build them in the universe's chosen ruleset (Tropelandia uses [D&D 5e](../../Rulesets)). Genesis does not change the system.
3. **Echo.** Write one **Echo**: a pull or instinct from the god's Domain that the mortal cannot explain (the sea-goddess who never feels cold; the god of bargains who always knows a price). Once per session, when acting on the Echo, you take a mild edge. In 5e that is advantage on one roll; in other systems a bonus die or similar.
4. **Fate.** The Scribe reads out the Pacts and gives each player a **Fate**: a consequence of a Pact their god made, felt by the mortal as luck, a debt, an enemy, a dream. The Scribe decides the shape; the player decides how the mortal copes with it.
5. **The Veil comes down.** Players now know only their mortal's life, the Proverbs and anything the mortal would plausibly know. If unsure, ask the Scribe: "does my mortal know this?" Players who remember their own god's creations should leave them unspoken in character. Clues to Seeds and Marks come from play.

### Resonance
When an incarnation meets something marked by their own god (a Seed, a Mark, one of their creations), the Scribe calls a **Resonance**: a dream, a chill, a coincidence. It is a story beat, not a mechanic, but it is also when the Echo is easiest to use.

### Reincarnation
When an incarnation dies or retires, the player's god is reborn in the next session as a new mortal. The player keeps a **Remnant**: one fact the old life learned, witnessed by the Scribe, that the new life dimly knows. Names and faces are gone; lessons stay. Echo and Fate carry over, and Fates can shift as Pacts are kept or broken. Great deeds become Events in the instance's log; mark the big ones `canon: proposed` for a Canon Merge.

## After the session: into the Omniverse
The sealed Ledger is the universe's founding canon.
1. Scaffold the universe: `python3 _rules/tools/new_universe.py "<Name>" --tone "<tone>" --ruleset <ruleset id> --maintainer <handle>`.
2. Each god becomes a `deity` note in `Pantheon/` (name, domains, symbol, tenets drawn from the First Word and Marks, `allies`/`rivals` from the Pacts).
3. Grand and Middling creations become the place tree, factions, eras, languages and holidays; Small ones become people, items, lore and events dated `primordial`.
4. Seeds stay in the Scribe's private notes (or as notes marked secret) until found.
5. Create an instance for the campaign and fork it at the frontier. Pacts become Fates in the characters' overlay notes.
6. Validate and build as usual (`validate.py`, `build_all.py`). An LLM can do steps 2 to 5 from the Ledger; see [Contributing with an LLM](Contributing%20with%20an%20LLM.md).

Prefer to do it yourselves? The [Note Wizard](../getting-started/The%20Note%20Wizard.md) turns each Ledger entry into a valid note: pick the kind, fill in the blanks, and save the whole basket into the new universe's folder.

## Scribe's checklist
- Before: print the handouts; pick a ruleset; decide the tone (a parody, grim, mythic); read the Opening aloud once to yourself.
- During: keep the Ledger current, call time at six minutes, keep the Veil, make sure every Act has an Anchor and a Mark, offer the clash rule instead of vetoing.
- Midway: remind quiet gods that Small Acts count, and that deals are available.
- Last round: collect the Seed Slips without reading them aloud.
- After: seal the Ledger; photograph it; start the universe.

## Variations
- **Short form (two hours):** two Acts per god (one Grand, one Seed), no deals, a 20-minute incarnation.
- **Mortal-first:** run the gods' round, then skip the Veil and let the gods play their mortals with full knowledge (a mythic, gods-among-us game).
- **Across sessions:** do one round at the start of each session of the campaign, so the world keeps growing. The gods are the players, and what they create arrives as events their mortals meet.
- **Rival houses:** with seven or eight gods, split into two houses of four that make separate halves of the world, then run a Joint Act to join them.
