# The Gameplay Loop

Become gods. Build a world together, and owe it a Doom for what you built. Hide a relic each. Go down as mortals who remember none of it, recover the relics, and use them together to soften the Doom. Retire. Come back as gods, build more, owe more, go down again. The world grows every cycle, the pantheon grows every cycle, and the history is all yours.

This page is the whole loop in one place. The phase guides are [Running a Genesis Session](../authoring/Running%20a%20Genesis%20Session.md) (the first cycle), [Running a Convocation](../authoring/Running%20a%20Convocation.md) (every later cycle) and [Running and Logging a Campaign](../authoring/Running%20and%20Logging%20a%20Campaign.md) (the mortal phases). The printable sheets are in [Genesis Session Handouts](../authoring/Genesis%20Session%20Handouts.md).

**A base way, not the right way.** Every phase below gives a *Base* and *Options*. Nothing is marked correct. Play it any way your table likes; the only rules that bite are the Omniverse's own note shapes, and only when you merge your world up (see the end of this page).

## The loop

```
Apotheosis → Genesis → Reckoning → Relics → Incarnation
     ↑                                            ↓
Convocation ← Retirement ← Long Game ← Doom ← Relic Quest
```

| # | Phase | At the table | What happens | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Apotheosis** | gods | Each player makes a god: Name, Domain, Sign, Voice, First Word. Costs nothing. | 30 min, first cycle only |
| 2 | **Genesis** | gods | The table makes the World together, then builds it by turns. Every creation adds Entropy to the debt. | 2 to 2.5 h first time; 30 to 60 min at a Convocation |
| 3 | **Reckoning** | gods | The gods spend the debt on the Doom: what will come, how big, how cruel. | 20 min |
| 4 | **Relics** | gods | Each god hides one relic for their future mortal. Costs nothing. | 5 min |
| 5 | **Incarnation** | players | Each player builds an avatar of their god. The Veil comes down. | 30 to 45 min |
| 6 | **Relic Quest** | avatars | The opening arc: recover the relics and wake them. | 1 to 3 sessions |
| 7 | **The Doom** | avatars | Join the relics and transform the Doom into something the world can live with. | 1 to 2 sessions |
| 8 | **Long Game** | avatars | Open play in the world they saved, for as long as the table likes. | any |
| 9 | **Retirement** | players | Each avatar's end: reborn, new chosen, return, or ascend. | 10 min |
| 10 | **Convocation** | gods | Read the Ledger, settle Pacts, welcome new gods, build more, Reckon again, skip time if you like, hide new relics. Then back to Incarnation. | 90 min |

A full first cycle is one four-hour Genesis Session plus a campaign. Every later cycle is one Convocation plus a campaign. A Convocation can run as the first hour of a session or as a short evening of its own.

## The Entropy economy

Creation leaks. Entropy is the price of a world: necessary and regrettable, nobody's mistake. The gods cannot refuse it; they can only choose the shape it takes, and then go down as mortals to soften it. Softening is the word: Entropy is spent, contained, vented or turned, never undone.

**Base.**

| Creation | Entropy |
|---|---|
| The World (a Joint Act of the whole table, before Round 1) | 5 |
| Grand (continent, people, law of reality, plane; any Joint Act) | 3 |
| Middling (region, country, city, faction, monster, era, event, language, holiday) | 2 |
| Small (person, item, place within a place, rumour, song, grudge) | 1 |
| Apotheosis, Relics, Proverbs, Canonizing a dead avatar, the Turning | 0 |

- The Scribe keeps the tally on the Ledger where everyone can see it. The table watches the threat grow as it builds and feels the choice between another continent and a tavern.
- Deals do not change weights: a gifted Act carries the giver's Mark and costs what it costs.
- At the Reckoning the gods spend the whole debt on the Doom (the menu below). A remainder too small to spend carries forward to the next Reckoning.
- The debt is always paid. Either the avatars vent it through the Convergence, or the Doom lands and the world pays in ruin.
- Building between campaigns raises a new debt, and a new debt is a new campaign. You can keep building and skip the campaign; the debt stands and grows.

**Options.**
- *Caveat spends 1.* When a Petition is refused, the Caveat the petitioner attaches spends 1 Entropy, since the cost was paid up front. Gives a refused god a real lever.
- *Deferral.* The gods may carry any amount forward on purpose, to keep this Doom small. Deferred Entropy grows by 1 for every cycle it waits.
- *Mercy.* For a first table or a young group, the Scribe may set the Doom one step smaller than the debt buys.
- *Prayer.* Once per campaign a god may answer one prayer with a Small act in the world. It costs 1, added to the next debt.
- *No economy.* Run without weights. The Reckoning picks the Doom freely. Everything else holds.

## The Doom

**Who shapes it.** Decide at the start of Genesis.
- *Council* (base): the whole table shapes it together at the Reckoning. Gods know all of it; avatars know none.
- *Doom-Keeper*: one god takes the duty, by default the Scribe's. The Keeper shapes the Doom privately after Genesis from the Ledger (the Caveats, the broken Pacts and the clashes are the best raw material) and decides how the relics join. The other gods know only their own relic, so even they discover the Convergence in play.
- *Mixed*: Council shapes the Doom, the Keeper decides the Convergence. The gods know what is coming, not how they will stop it.

**The menu.** Spend the debt. Pick one Scale and one Form (required), then Features until the debt is gone.

| Scale (pick one) | What is at risk | Cost | Convergence terms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scar | one place | 4 | join the relics anywhere |
| Blight | a region | 8 | join the relics anywhere |
| Scourge | a nation | 12 | at one place |
| Cataclysm | a continent | 18 | at one place, at one moment |
| Unmaking | the world | 24 | at one place, at one moment, and one bearer pays a price |

**Form (pick one, cost 3):** fire, flood, plague, war, famine, winter, silence, rot, madness, or **a Domain turned**: the Doom wears one god's Domain gone wrong, and that god's avatar feels it most.

| Feature (each once) | Cost | What it does to the campaign |
|---|---|---|
| A Face | 4 | The Doom has a villain or herald who wants it to come. |
| A Clock | 2 | It arrives at a known time; the avatars race it. |
| Servants | 3 | A cult, an army or a court serves it. |
| Spread | 3 | It grows while ignored: every session it goes unaddressed, it takes more. |
| A Hunger | 3 | It wants one specific thing (a person, a place, a relic) and goes there. |
| A Mask | 2 | It is not what it seems; the public face is wrong. |
| Roots | 2 | It is tied to one creation the gods love, which cannot be saved without saving the Doom. |
| Echoes | 2 | It has happened before; there are ruins and a record. |

A typical first table owes about 35: a Scourge with every Feature, a Cataclysm with most, or an Unmaking with a few. Wide and simple, or narrow and cruel.

**The public face.** Whatever the gods decide, the Scribe writes two versions: the true Doom (private) and what mortals believe about it (a prophecy, a rumour, a sermon). The avatars start with the public face only.

**The Convergence transforms; it never deletes.** When the relics are joined on the Doom's terms, the Doom becomes something the world can live with. The gods choose the shape at the next Convocation, or the Keeper decides in play:
- *A season*: it returns, smaller, on a cycle.
- *A scar*: one place stays ruined.
- *A seal*: it is bound somewhere, with a door that should stay shut.
- *A cost*: someone or something is spent.
- *A new thing*: it becomes a people, a faction, a monster, a god.

**If it lands.** If the avatars fail, or the table skips the campaign and lets it come, the Doom happens at its Scale. The Scribe applies it to the world at the next Convocation, in ruins, refugees, a lost nation, an age ending. The debt is paid either way.

## The people

- **Gods** are the players at a Genesis or a Convocation. A god is a `deity` note in the pantheon.
- **Avatars** are the players in a campaign. Base: an avatar is the god incarnate, with no memory of it, and feels the god's Domain as an **Echo**. Option: a **chosen one** instead, a mortal the god picked rather than became; the Echo is a blessing, the rules are identical, the fiction is yours.
- **Dormant gods** are gods whose player is playing someone else this cycle. They stay in the pantheon, and the Scribe speaks for them if they must act.
- **New gods** arrive at any Convocation: a new player, a player swapping gods, or an ascended avatar (below). Each takes one Arrival Act (Grand, 3) and says a First Word.
- **The Scribe** runs the table. Three ways to play it: *Scribe only* (runs, creates nothing); *god only* (creates at Genesis and Convocations, takes the Doom-Keeper duty by default, never incarnates, DMs the mortal phases); *god and avatar* for small tables, with their own relic on the honor system.
- **The Veil** cuts both ways. Avatars know only what mortals know: the Proverbs, the public face of the Doom, their own lives. Gods cannot touch the world between Convocations except through their avatars. That is why anyone incarnates at all.

## Retirement

An avatar's story ends when they die, or when the player steps away from them at the end of a campaign. Mid-campaign, a dead avatar's god is reborn at once as a new avatar who joins the party (the relic wakes again for them, since it is the same god). At the end of a campaign each player picks one end:

| End | What it means | What it writes |
|---|---|---|
| **Reborn** | The god returns later as a new avatar. | the old avatar's `to:` date; a **Remnant** (one fact the old life learned, carried into the next) |
| **New chosen** | The avatar lives on as an NPC; the god picks a different mortal next time. | the avatar stays, now `player_character: false` |
| **Return** | The avatar's life closes and the god simply resumes. | `to:` date; a **Legend** (what mortals say about them, as `lore`) |
| **Ascend** | The avatar joins the pantheon as a new, lesser god. Their player may play them as their god next cycle, leaving the old god dormant. | a new `deity` note, domains drawn from what they did |

Whatever the end, the player writes a Remnant and a Legend. Echo and Fate carry to the next avatar; Fates shift as Pacts are kept or broken.

**Option: Domain drift.** At a Convocation, if an avatar's deeds leaned one way, the god may add an aspect to their Domain (the god of Tides whose avatar was a smuggler becomes the god of Tides and Smuggled Things). The deity note's `domains` list grows.

## Between cycles: the Convocation

The gods reconvene after a campaign (or before the next one). In order: the Scribe reads the Ledger and the Veil lifts for the gods; the last Doom's outcome is written; Pacts come due; new gods arrive; one or two building rounds, with weights; a Reckoning for the new debt; the Turning (an optional time-skip of a season, a generation or an age); new relics. Then Incarnation again. It is also the moment the campaign's events merge into the universe's canon. The full procedure is in [Running a Convocation](../authoring/Running%20a%20Convocation.md).

## What must hold when you merge up

Locally, none of the above is enforced. If your world joins the multiverse, or your campaign's events merge into a shared universe's canon, these hold:
- Notes validate: every creation is a note of a known kind with its required fields (`python3 _rules/tools/validate.py . --quiet`).
- Ids are permanent and names are unique. A retired avatar keeps their id; an ascended one gets a new `deity` id.
- Dates are on the universe's own calendar. A Turning advances the calendar and, for an age, opens a new `era`.
- Canon is the maintainer's call. Events from your instance go up as `canon: proposed`, and the universe's maintainer accepts or rejects them. A Doom that landed is an event like any other.
- The loop's vocabulary is optional. Another universe may run no Entropy at all. Only what lands in notes matters.

## Where each phase lands in the repository

| Phase | Note kinds | Notes on fields |
|---|---|---|
| Apotheosis | `deity` in `Pantheon/` | `domains` is the Domain; `symbol` the Sign; `tenets` from the First Word and the Marks; `allies` and `rivals` from Pacts; `from: primordial` |
| Genesis | places, `culture`, `faction`, `monster`, `era`, `event`, `language`, `holiday`, `person`, `item`, `lore` | Genesis creations date `primordial` or year 1 of the calendar |
| Reckoning | `lore` (public face, `category: prophecy`, `truth: half` or so) and, privately, a `quest` for the campaign | The quest's `stages` are the relics and the Convergence; `on_success` is the transformation, `on_failure` is the Doom landing |
| Relics | `item` | Keep relic notes in a private instance (`visibility: private`) until found; `mechanics` for the alone-power, `history` for who hid it; move them to the universe's `Items/` when they are known |
| Incarnation | `person` under the instance's campaign | `player_character: true`, `player`, `instance`, `sheet`; Echo, Fate and Remnant in the body or `secret` |
| Relic Quest, Doom, Long Game | `session` logs, `event` | big ones `canon: proposed` for the merge |
| Retirement | `person` (`to:` date), `lore` (`category: legend`), `deity` for an ascended avatar | |
| Convocation | the Canon Merge (`merge_instance.py`), new creations, `event` for the Doom's outcome, `era` after an age | the Ledger's Entropy tally can live in the instance's `Campaign.md` `arc` |

The [Note Wizard](../getting-started/The%20Note%20Wizard.md) writes every kind above from a form, and its chips show each kind's Genesis size and Entropy weight.
